Investing in India from Singapore

Treaty changes since 2017, Singapore's treatment of foreign gains, fund access and practical setup for Singapore-based NRIs and OCIs.

Last reviewed 25 Nov 2026.

Indian mutual funds
Broad access; most fund houses accept Singapore residents
Singapore tax on capital gains
Generally not taxed for individual investors
India–Singapore treaty
Gains on Indian shares acquired from 1 April 2017 are taxable in India
CPF / SRS
Generally cannot be used for Indian mutual funds directly
Digital KYC from abroad
Yes, from 3 Dec 2026 (Singapore is FATF-compliant)

Singapore residents generally pay no local tax on investment gains, so — as in the UAE — Indian withholding is the main cost to manage.

The treaty, briefly

The India–Singapore treaty was amended in 2016. Gains on Indian shares acquired on or after 1 April 2017 are taxable in India. Dividends and interest can benefit from treaty rates if you provide a Singapore certificate of residence from IRAS and Form 10F.

What works

  • Indian mutual funds online from most fund houses.
  • Direct Indian shares via an NRI demat.
  • NRE and FCNR deposits.
  • GIFT City funds and deposits in US dollars.

Practical points

  • Your overseas address proof should be Singapore-issued (tenancy agreement, bank statement or utility bill).
  • Employment Pass holders should keep a copy of the pass with their KYC documents.
  • File an Indian return to recover excess TDS on redemptions.

General information only, not tax or investment advice. Tax rules in Singapore and India change; confirm with a qualified cross-border professional.
