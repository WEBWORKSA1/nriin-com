How to open an NRI demat and trading account: step by step

Bank-linked 3-in-1 or discount broker, repatriable or non-repatriable, the documents you need and how long it takes in 2026.

Last reviewed 25 Nov 2026 by the NRIin editorial desk. 7 minute read.

To buy and hold Indian shares from abroad you need three linked accounts: a bank account, a demat account that holds the shares and a trading account that places orders.

Repatriable or non-repatriable

A repatriable demat (often labelled NRE) is funded from your designated NRE account and lets you send sale proceeds abroad. A non-repatriable demat (NRO) holds shares bought with Indian income or shares you owned before becoming non-resident. Many investors need both.

Documents

  • Passport (at least six months' validity) and visa, residence permit or OCI card
  • PAN card
  • Overseas address proof (utility bill, bank statement or government ID with address)
  • Recent photograph and signature
  • Cancelled cheque or statement of the linked NRE/NRO account
  • FATCA/CRS self-certification

Copies must be attested by a notary, an Indian embassy or consulate, or an authorised official of an overseas branch of an Indian bank, unless you complete a fully digital KYC (available from abroad from 3 December 2026 for FATF-compliant countries).

The process

  1. Open or re-designate your NRE and NRO savings accounts.
  2. Have the bank designate the repatriable account for listed-equity investment.
  3. Submit the broker's account-opening form with attested documents.
  4. Complete in-person or video verification.
  5. Receive your client ID, demat account number and login; transfer funds and place your first delivery order.

Before you start, run the document check — it flags the attestation and name-mismatch errors that cause most rejections.

Questions people ask

Can I keep my old resident demat account?

No. Once you become non-resident, holdings must move to an NRO (non-repatriable) demat. Open the new account and transfer the shares — it is not a sale.

Which is better, a bank 3-in-1 or a discount broker?

Bank-linked accounts are simpler because the bank handles the designated account and reporting. Discount brokers are cheaper per trade but need a partner bank for the designated account.

How long does it take?

With complete documents, one to three weeks is typical. Courier delays and attestation mistakes are the usual causes of longer waits.
