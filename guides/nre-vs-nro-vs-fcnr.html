NRE vs NRO vs FCNR: which account to use for investing in India

How the three non-resident accounts differ on funding, tax and repatriation, and which one to link to your demat and mutual fund folios.

Last reviewed 25 Nov 2026 by the NRIin editorial desk. 6 minute read.

Your bank account decides two things before you buy a single share: whether you can take the money back out of India, and how the income is taxed. Choose it on purpose.

Which account to link for investing

Stocks you may want to sell and take abroad

Fund the purchase from an NRE account (or by inward remittance). Since the June 2026 FEMA amendment, individual investors resident outside India must designate a repatriable rupee account used only for listed-equity investments — banks and brokers still often call this the “NRE-PIS” account. See PIS after June 2026.

Stocks bought with Indian income

Use an NRO account and an NRO (non-repatriable) demat. These investments are treated much like domestic investments, but sale proceeds return to NRO and are subject to the NRO repatriation limit.

Mutual funds

No separate PIS designation is needed. Register the NRE account as the bank mandate if you want redemptions to be repatriable; use NRO if the money came from Indian income.

Common mistake: keeping an old resident savings account after moving abroad. Banks must re-designate it as NRO once you become non-resident, and SIPs or folios linked to it can be rejected at redemption. Tell your bank promptly.

Joint holding rules in one paragraph

NRE and FCNR accounts can be held jointly with another non-resident, or with a resident close relative on a “former or survivor” basis. NRO accounts can be held jointly with residents. For PIS-designated accounts each joint holder normally needs their own designation, so many investors keep investment accounts in a single name.

Questions people ask

Can I invest from an NRO account?

Yes. Investments funded from NRO are made on a non-repatriation basis (or, for current income, can be repatriated within the NRO limits). Many NRIs hold both an NRE-linked and an NRO-linked demat.

Can I move money from NRO to NRE?

Yes, within the USD 1 million per financial year limit for NRO balances, after taxes are paid and usually with Form 15CA/15CB.

Is NRE interest really tax-free?

Interest on NRE and FCNR(B) deposits is exempt from Indian tax for individuals who qualify as non-residents. It may still be taxable in your country of residence.
