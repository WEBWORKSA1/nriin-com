FATCA and CRS declarations for NRIs, explained

Why every Indian bank, broker and fund house asks for a FATCA/CRS self-certification, what to declare, and what happens to the information.

Last reviewed 25 Nov 2026 by the NRIin editorial desk. 5 minute read.

FATCA (a US law) and CRS (an OECD standard adopted by more than 100 countries, including India) both require financial institutions to find out where their customers pay tax and report accounts held by foreign tax residents.

What you are asked to declare

  • Every country where you are tax resident, with your taxpayer identification number for each (for example a US SSN, Canadian SIN or UK UTR).
  • Whether you are a US person.
  • Your country of birth and citizenship.
  • For entities: whether the entity is a financial institution or a passive entity with controlling persons.

Where you will meet it

At account opening and whenever your circumstances change — with your bank, your broker and depository participant, and each mutual fund registrar (CAMS or KFintech). Updating it through MF Central covers many folios at once.

Why US persons face extra friction

FATCA reporting on US persons is detailed and carries penalties for the institution if it gets it wrong. That cost is the main reason many Indian fund houses decline US residents — see mutual funds for US and Canada NRIs.

Your declaration must match your other records. A FATCA form showing UK tax residence while your KYC shows a US address is a common cause of an account being put on hold.

Questions people ask

What if I skip the FATCA declaration?

Indian institutions are required to restrict or close accounts without a valid self-certification. Expect transactions to be blocked until you provide it.

Is a green-card holder a US person for FATCA?

Yes. US citizens, green-card holders and anyone who meets the US substantial-presence test are US persons.

Does India share my data with my home country?

Yes, for reportable accounts. Information goes to the Indian tax authority, which exchanges it with your country of tax residence under FATCA or CRS agreements.

Sources

  • Income Tax Department — FATCA/CRS
  • OECD — Common Reporting Standard
