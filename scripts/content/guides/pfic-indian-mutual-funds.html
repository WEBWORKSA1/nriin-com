If you are a US citizen, green-card holder or US tax resident, the most expensive mistake you can make with Indian investments is buying an Indian mutual fund without understanding PFIC.

What a PFIC is

A Passive Foreign Investment Company is a non-US company that earns mostly passive income or holds mostly passive assets. Almost every pooled investment vehicle outside the US qualifies — including Indian mutual funds, Indian-listed ETFs, ULIPs and pooled GIFT City funds.

What it costs you

  • Form 8621 for each fund, each year. The IRS's own time estimate for the form runs to many hours; preparers commonly charge roughly US$100–300 per form.
  • The default “excess distribution” method spreads gains back over your holding period, taxes them at the highest ordinary rate for each year and adds an interest charge. Gains are never taxed at long-term capital gains rates.
  • The alternatives are imperfect. A Qualified Electing Fund election needs an annual statement Indian funds rarely provide. Mark-to-market taxes unrealised gains every year as ordinary income.
  • An unfiled Form 8621 can keep your return open to IRS review well beyond the normal three-year window.

The other two forms you cannot skip

  • FBAR (FinCEN 114): required if the combined maximum value of your foreign financial accounts exceeded US$10,000 at any time in the year.
  • Form 8938: for taxpayers living in the US, required if specified foreign assets exceed US$50,000 at year end or US$75,000 at any time (single), or US$100,000 / US$150,000 (married filing jointly). Higher thresholds apply if you live abroad.
