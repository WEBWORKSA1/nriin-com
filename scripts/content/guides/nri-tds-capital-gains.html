From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act. The rates for listed equity did not change, but section numbers and some forms did — and the old “previous year/assessment year” pair is now a single “tax year”.

How withholding works for NRIs

Payments to non-residents fall under section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (the successor to section 195). In practice:

  • Mutual funds: the fund house deducts tax on the capital gain at redemption.
  • Shares: your broker or bank computes and deducts tax on each sale before proceeds reach your account.
  • Dividends and NRO interest: withheld at the domestic rate unless you give a Tax Residency Certificate and treaty declaration.

Why NRIs often overpay — and how to get it back

  • Withholding usually ignores the ₹1.25 lakh annual exemption for long-term equity gains.
  • Losses on other trades in the year are not netted off by the withholding agent.
  • Treaty rates on dividends and interest only apply if you submitted documents in advance.

File an Indian return for the tax year to claim the excess. Refunds go to the bank account validated on the e-filing portal — usually your NRO account.

Need a lower deduction up front? You can apply for a lower or nil withholding certificate when you expect tax withheld to exceed your actual liability (commonly used on property sales). See treaty relief and lower TDS.

And in your country of residence

Most countries tax residents on worldwide income and give a credit for Indian tax. Keep the fund house or broker tax statement and your Indian return; your local preparer will need both. US persons should read the PFIC guide.
