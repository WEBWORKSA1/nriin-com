India has tax treaties with more than 90 countries. They rarely make Indian income tax-free, but they can cut withholding on dividends and interest — if your paperwork reaches the payer before the payment.

The three documents

  1. Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from your country of residence — for example IRS Form 6166 in the US, a certificate from the CRA in Canada, HMRC in the UK or the Federal Tax Authority in the UAE.
  2. Form 10F (filed electronically on the Indian e-filing portal), giving the details the TRC does not show. Form numbering is being aligned to the Income-tax Act, 2025, so your payer may refer to the successor form.
  3. A self-declaration that you have no permanent establishment in India and are the beneficial owner of the income — most fund houses and banks have their own template.

Who needs them

  • Each fund house that pays you dividends (send through the registrar, CAMS or KFintech).
  • Your bank, for NRO interest.
  • Companies paying dividends on your shares — usually via the registrar's online treaty-form facility before the record date.

Lower or nil withholding certificates

When tax withheld would clearly exceed your actual liability — a property sale where the gain is small, or a large redemption offset by losses — you can apply to your assessing officer for a lower or nil deduction certificate (formerly Form 13 under section 197). Apply well before the transaction; processing takes weeks, and the certificate names the specific payer.

Claiming credit at home

Treaty relief in India is half the job. In your country of residence, claim a foreign tax credit for Indian tax actually paid — Form 1116 in the US, the foreign tax credit on your T1 in Canada, foreign tax credit relief in the UK. You need the Indian withholding certificate and, ideally, your Indian return.

Not tax advice. Treaty articles differ country by country; a cross-border preparer can tell you which rates apply to you.
