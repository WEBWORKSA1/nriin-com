If you live in the United States or Canada, most Indian fund houses will reject your application at KYC — not because Indian law forbids it, but because servicing you costs them more than they want to spend.

Why the door is mostly shut

  • FATCA. US law requires foreign financial institutions to report US account holders to the IRS. Many fund houses decided the reporting burden was not worth it.
  • Securities registration. US and Canadian rules on selling securities to local residents mean a fund house could be seen as marketing unregistered products there.
  • Result: roughly a dozen fund houses take US/Canada investors, some only on paper forms, and the list changes without notice.

Fund houses reported to accept US and Canada NRIs

Two independent lists (Zerodha Varsity, September 2025, and Dealplexus, June 2026) agree on these eleven: SBI, ICICI Prudential, Aditya Birla Sun Life, UTI, Nippon India, Canara Robeco, HSBC (formerly L&T), PPFAS, Sundaram, Tata and Quant. The 2026 list says SBI and ICICI Prudential take US/Canada investors offline only, with a signed declaration, and reports Aditya Birla Sun Life accepting online onboarding with a FATCA declaration.

How to invest, step by step

  1. Make sure your KYC is Validated and your tax status is NRI. Use the KYC checker.
  2. Choose a fund house from the accepting list and download its NRI application form and FATCA/CRS declaration.
  3. Pay from an NRE account (repatriable) or NRO account (non-repatriable). Third-party payments are rejected.
  4. If the fund house requires paper forms, sign, attach the attested documents and courier them to its registrar (CAMS or KFintech) in India.
  5. Keep the acknowledgement. Your folio should appear on MF Central within a few days.

For US persons, read this first. Indian mutual funds are generally treated as Passive Foreign Investment Companies (PFICs). The annual Form 8621 filing and the default tax method can wipe out much of your return. Read PFIC and Indian mutual funds and run the PFIC check.

Alternatives if your preferred fund is not available

  • Direct Indian shares through a non-resident demat account (not a PFIC for US persons when they are operating companies).
  • India-focused ETFs listed in the US or Canada.
  • GIFT City funds and portfolio management services in US dollars — see GIFT City for NRIs.
