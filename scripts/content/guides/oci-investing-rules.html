For investing in Indian securities, an Overseas Citizen of India is treated much like a Non-Resident Indian. The differences are mostly in paperwork and in property.

What OCI cardholders can do

  • Open NRE, NRO and FCNR(B) accounts.
  • Buy listed shares on the stock exchange through a repatriable or non-repatriable demat account.
  • Invest in mutual funds (subject to each fund house's policy for your country of residence).
  • Apply in IPOs, buy government securities and bonds open to non-residents, and invest in GIFT City products.
  • Buy residential and commercial property.

What they cannot do

  • Buy agricultural land, plantation property or farmhouses (inheritance is treated differently).
  • Trade intraday or short sell in the cash market, the same as NRIs.

The extra paperwork

Expect to provide your foreign passport, OCI card, PAN, overseas address proof and a FATCA/CRS self-certification. If your passport shows a place of birth outside India, the OCI card is what links you to the non-resident Indian investment framework.

Since June 2026, foreign nationals without OCI can also buy listed Indian shares through the same route — but OCI cardholders keep their advantages in property and in established onboarding processes at banks and brokers.

Tax residence is separate

Whether India taxes your worldwide income depends on how many days you spend in India and your Indian income — not on whether you hold an OCI card. Use the residential status calculator for the current tax year.
