For decades, only NRIs and OCI cardholders could buy listed Indian shares directly on the stock exchange, through the Portfolio Investment Scheme. In June 2026 that changed.

What changed in June 2026

The Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) (Third Amendment) Rules, 2026, effective 12 June 2026, and RBI's matching reporting regulations effective 13 June 2026, did three things:

  1. Opened the route to every individual living outside India — not only NRIs and OCIs, but foreign nationals with no Indian origin.
  2. Doubled the individual limit from 5% to 10% of a company's paid-up equity capital.
  3. Raised the aggregate limit for all such individuals from 10% to 24%, without needing a special resolution by the company.

Sector caps still apply, and private sector banks keep stricter limits.

What your setup looks like now

  • One designated repatriable rupee account (NRE, or funded by inward remittance) used only for these listed-equity investments.
  • A repatriable demat account linked to it, plus a trading account with a broker that serves non-residents.
  • Your authorised dealer bank reports every purchase and sale to RBI and tracks your holding against the limits.
  • For investments with Indian income, a separate NRO account and non-repatriable demat. Non-repatriable investments outside the bank sector are treated much like domestic investment.

Trading rules that still apply

  • Delivery only. Intraday trading and short selling in the cash market are not allowed for non-residents.
  • Derivatives are possible only from non-repatriable (NRO) funds through a clearing member with a custodial participant code. See IPOs, F&O and intraday.
  • Breach of 10%: sell the excess within 5 trading days and inform the company and depositories through your bank within 7 trading days.
  • Investors from countries sharing a land border with India need government approval for control, majority ownership or significant beneficial ownership.

What has not changed: capital gains are still taxed in India at the listed-equity rates, with tax withheld on sale. See our TDS guide and the tax estimator.
