Canadian residents face the same fund-house restrictions as Americans — driven by Canadian securities rules rather than FATCA — but a simpler home tax picture, because Canada does not have a PFIC regime.

What works for Canadian residents

  • Direct Indian shares through an NRI demat and trading account.
  • Mutual funds from the fund houses that accept Canadian investors — check the directory.
  • India ETFs listed in Canada or the US, held in a Canadian brokerage account, including TFSAs and RRSPs where eligible.
  • NRE and FCNR deposits (interest taxable in Canada).

Canadian reporting

  • Form T1135: required if the total cost of your specified foreign property — Indian bank deposits, shares and fund units — exceeded CAD 100,000 at any time in the year. Penalties for late filing are significant.
  • Income and gains: report in Canadian dollars at the applicable exchange rates; Canada taxes 50% of capital gains under its general inclusion rules.
  • Foreign tax credit: claim Indian tax paid, subject to treaty limits.
  • Indian mutual funds are structured as trusts; ask a cross-border accountant whether any additional trust-related reporting applies to your holdings.

Indian side

Expect tax withheld on sales and dividends. A Canadian residency certificate from the CRA plus Form 10F lets you claim treaty rates on dividends and interest. File an Indian return to recover any excess TDS.
