Australian residents are taxed on worldwide income, so Indian gains are taxed twice on paper — in India first, then in Australia with a credit for the Indian tax.

How the two systems interact

  • India withholds tax on your gains and dividends.
  • Australia taxes the same gain; if you held the asset for 12 months or more, generally only 50% of the gain is assessable.
  • You claim a foreign income tax offset for Indian tax paid, limited to the Australian tax on that income.
  • Keep records in rupees and Australian dollars with the exchange rate on each transaction date.

What works

  • Indian mutual funds from most fund houses.
  • Direct Indian shares through an NRI demat.
  • India ETFs listed on the ASX for simpler local reporting.
  • NRE and FCNR deposits (interest assessable in Australia).

Indian side

Obtain a certificate of residency from the ATO and file Form 10F to claim treaty rates on dividends and interest. File an Indian return to recover excess TDS on capital gains.
