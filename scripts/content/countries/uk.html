UK residents can invest in nearly every Indian fund house, but the UK's offshore fund rules decide how much of the gain you keep.

The offshore fund question

Under UK rules, gains on an overseas fund are taxed as capital gains only if the fund has reporting fund status with HMRC. Most Indian mutual funds do not, so gains on disposal are usually taxed as income at your marginal rate. Check HMRC's published list of reporting funds before you invest.

Routes UK residents use

  • Direct Indian shares through an NRI demat — UK capital gains rules apply to individual shares.
  • Indian mutual funds directly, accepting income-tax treatment on gains unless the fund reports.
  • UK-listed India funds and ETFs with reporting status, including inside an ISA or SIPP.
  • NRE and FCNR deposits — interest taxable in the UK.

Non-doms after April 2025

The remittance basis ended on 6 April 2025. Long-standing residents are taxed on worldwide income and gains as they arise; new arrivals may qualify for the four-year foreign income and gains regime. Take advice if you previously kept Indian income offshore under the old rules.

Indian side

Indian TDS applies on sales and dividends. Get a certificate of residence from HMRC and file Form 10F to claim treaty rates on dividends and interest, and claim foreign tax credit relief in your UK return.
